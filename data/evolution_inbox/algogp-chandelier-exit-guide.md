It trails a stop a multiple of ATR below the highest high, giving trends room to breathe. 4 min readIntermediateUpdated October 14, 2026 Quick answer The chandelier exit sets a long trailing stop at the highest high of the last n periods minus a multiple of ATR, typically 22 periods and 3x ATR(22). Shorts mirror it from the lowest low. Because it scales with volatility, it stays wide in volatile markets and tightens in calm ones, which makes it a robust exit for trend-following strategies. At a glance CreatorChuck LeBeau Long stopHighest high(22) − 3 × ATR(22) Short stopLowest low(22) + 3 × ATR(22) Adapts toVolatility Best forExits on trend and breakout systems Key takeaways Exits often matter more than entries for trend strategies; the chandelier is one of the most robust. Anchoring to the highest high means the stop only rises, like any trailing stop. Multipliers of 2.5 to 3.5 suit daily swing and position trades; lower multipliers exit sooner. It can be swapped onto almost any entry rule for a fair comparison. Gaps through the stop still fill at the open price. Frequently asked questions What are the standard chandelier exit settings? A 22-period lookback (about a trading month) and 3 times ATR(22). Many traders test multipliers between 2.5 and 4. Can I use the chandelier exit for entries? It is designed as an exit, but a close back above a short chandelier stop is sometimes used as a trend-change entry signal. Why use ATR instead of a percentage? ATR measures how much the market actually moves, so the stop sits outside normal noise for that market and regime. A fixed percentage ignores volatility. Key terms in this guide Chandelier exitThe chandelier exit is a trailing stop set a multiple of ATR below the highest high since entry (for longs), hanging from the high like a chandelier. Trailing stopA trailing stop is a stop level that moves in the direction of a profitable trade by a fixed amount, percentage, or volatility multiple, but never moves back. Average True Range (ATR)Average True Range measures volatility as the average of each bar’s true range, including gaps; it is the standard tool for setting stops and sizing positions. Anchoring biasAnchoring is relying too heavily on an initial reference point, such as your entry price or a past high, when making decisions. Stop-loss orderA stop-loss order becomes a market order once price reaches a specified stop level, used to cap the loss on a position automatically. Parabolic SARParabolic SAR (stop and reverse) places dots below price in uptrends and above in downtrends, accelerating toward price over time to act as a trailing stop. SupertrendSupertrend is an ATR-based trend line that sits below price in uptrends and above it in downtrends, flipping when price closes through it. Swap (rollover) and carrySwap, or rollover, is the interest credited or debited when an FX or CFD position is held past the daily rollover, reflecting the rate difference between the two currencies. Profit factorProfit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss; above 1 means the strategy made money, and robust systems typically show 1.3 to 2 after costs. Maximum drawdownMaximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in equity over a period, the most-cited measure of how painful a strategy has been. DrawdownA drawdown is the decline in account or strategy equity from a peak to a subsequent trough, measured in percentage or currency. Win rateWin rate is the percentage of trades that close with a profit; on its own it says nothing about profitability without the size of wins and losses. Donchian ChannelA Donchian Channel plots the highest high and lowest low of the last n bars; a break of the upper band is the classic trend-following breakout entry. BreakoutA breakout is price moving decisively beyond a support or resistance level or out of a consolidation, often with expanding volume, signalling a potential new move.
